# EXF-070 — Championship: the top three and the winner

**Node:** `EXF-070` · **Round:** R7 · **Roles:** Orchestrator, Curator
**Stopping condition:** the top three, the winner, the dissent, and the runner-up reversal conditions are recorded and internally consistent.

**Verdict: ADAPT.** No single scouted candidate wins outright. The winner is a hybrid of
an existing NASA technology and an evidence mechanism the applicant already owns.

---

## 🥇 Winner — Corrosion evidence for coastal launch and energy infrastructure

*Working title: **Sealed Coat**. Entrant T1. Weighted score 8.57.*

**The idea in one sentence.** License NASA's Kennedy Space Center smart coating — which
releases corrosion *indicators* and *inhibitors* from microcapsules, so the material
detects corrosion in its early stages, inhibits it, and can repair the coating — and sell
it not as a paint but as a **tamper-evident corrosion record** for the launch pads,
pipelines, port steel and LNG tankage of the Rio Grande Valley.

**Why it wins.**

- **It is the archetype the sponsor's pipeline was built to produce.** ExF holds a Space
  Act Agreement with NASA JSC and describes itself as a concierge of NASA technologies for
  Brownsville. Its pitch competition is historically won by graduates commercialising
  *licensed NASA technologies*. This entry is not adjacent to that pipeline; it is the
  thing the pipeline is for.
- **The geography is the product's best argument.** Starbase sits on a ridge through
  tidal wetlands, in salt spray and high humidity, and SpaceX is now laying an eight-mile
  gas pipeline from the Port of Brownsville with an on-site liquefaction plant. Coastal
  launch structures, buried and above-ground pipe, port steel and cryogenic tankage —
  every one of them is a corrosion asset, and they are all within thirty miles of the
  sponsor's office.
- **It joins the two industries the sponsor's money is about.** Rockets & Rigs exists to
  find problems common to space and oil & gas. Corrosion in a marine environment is that
  problem, almost definitionally, and the DOE prize that funds Rockets & Rigs was awarded
  for exactly this kind of cross-industry commercialisation.
- **It demonstrates in ten minutes.** Hand a judging panel two coupons from a salt-fog
  cabinet: one bare, one coated and visibly indicating where corrosion started. Then show
  the tamper-evident inspection receipt binding that photograph, that date, that asset and
  that independently re-executed check. Nothing else in the field can be shown working on
  a table.
- **It converts the applicant's real asset into differentiation rather than the whole
  pitch.** Round one vetoed the applicant's standalone product for having no regional
  footprint. Attached to a licensed NASA coating on Brownsville infrastructure, the same
  receipt engine becomes the reason this entry is not just another coatings reseller.
- **The next cheque is obvious and large.** A licensed NASA technology infused into a
  commercial product is the strongest possible NASA SBIR framing, and the Department of
  Defense runs a dedicated Corrosion Prevention and Control programme that funds precisely
  this problem.

**The business, stated plainly.** The coating is the sensor. The revenue is the record.
Asset owners pay per inspection for a defensible corrosion history they can hand to an
insurer, a regulator, or a buyer — *prove this asset was sound on this date*. Incumbent
coatings manufacturers sell a can and become channel partners, not competitors.

**Its real weakness, stated without spin.** Coating qualification into an owner's
approved-products specification takes years. The entry must earn early revenue as an
inspection service layered onto existing coated assets, not as a coating replacement. Any
pitch that skips this will be caught by the first judge who has worked in industrial
maintenance.

## 🥈 Runner-up — Tamper-evident compliance evidence platform

*Entrant T3. Weighted score 7.03.*

Continuous, sealed, independently re-executed environmental and safety evidence for
launch and energy operators filing to FAA, TCEQ and PHMSA. **The highest execution
readiness in the field** — the applicant could demonstrate a working version this week —
and the strongest capital efficiency.

It loses to T1 on the two criteria that decide this sponsor: it has no NASA
technology-transfer anchor (4/10), and its regional economic footprint is a software team
rather than an industry (6/10). It also carries a buyer problem T1 does not: T1 sells an
asset owner protection, while T3 sells them proof that can be used against them.

**Keep it.** It is the fallback if the NASA license fails, and it is a better standalone
company than its score here suggests — the score measures fit to *this* sponsor.

## 🥉 Third — Cryogenic boil-off recovery for Starbase and the Port

*Entrant T2. Weighted score 6.59.*

Capture and reliquefy methane vapour currently lost at launch-site cryogenic operations
and at Port of Brownsville LNG facilities. **The purest expression of Rockets & Rigs in
the whole field** — a perfect 10 on the space × energy intersection, 9 on regional impact,
and the best DOE funding story of any entrant.

It is third because it is unexecutable by this applicant at this scale: nothing exists
today, capital efficiency scores 2/10, it cannot be demonstrated at a pitch, and its
anchor customer is the most aggressive vertical integrator in the industry and is already
building its own liquefaction plant.

**Worth entering only with a cryogenics co-founder.** As a partnership pitch — this
applicant's evidence layer plus a process engineer's rig — it would score materially
higher, and that recombination is the most promising thing in the losers' bracket.

## Recorded dissent

A verdict without dissent is not reviewable. Three objections stand:

1. **Curator.** T1's perfect 10 on NASA fit is *entirely conditional* on the KSC coating
   being licensable in 2026. The patent is documented as allowed and the technology is
   published on NASA's transfer portal, but availability was not confirmed from this
   session. If it is exclusively licensed or withdrawn, T1's NASA fit falls to roughly 4
   and **T3 wins the recomputed board.** This is the single most load-bearing unverified
   fact in the tournament.
2. **Architect.** T2 is the truest Rockets & Rigs entry. If the 2026 panel is weighted
   toward DOE-aligned energy judges rather than NASA-transfer criteria, T2's domain
   perfection could outweigh its demonstration and capital weaknesses. The rubric's
   weighting of NASA fit above the intersection is defensible but not certain.
3. **Builder.** T1 requires the applicant to operate in materials science, which is
   outside their demonstrated competence. The evidence layer is theirs; the coating is
   not. Without a materials partner, T1 degrades into T3 wearing a NASA logo — and a
   judging panel will find that out.

## Conditions that reverse the verdict

The runner-up wins instead if **any** of these turns out to be true:

- **R-1.** The NASA KSC corrosion coating, or an equivalent NASA corrosion-sensing
  technology, is not available for license.
- **R-2.** No materials or coatings partner is secured within the programme timeline.
- **R-3.** The competition is closed to entrants outside an EIRA or NASA Tech Trek cohort
  and the applicant cannot join one in time — T3 is the better standalone entry because it
  needs no license and no partner to demonstrate.

Each condition maps to a confirmation in `09_SUBMISSION_PLAN.md`. Confirm `R-1` first; it
is cheap to check and it decides the answer.
