# GENERICPROMPT — HIVE MIND OS SELF-OPERATING AUTONOMY TOURNAMENT + EXECUTION OS

## SUBJECT

The complete live codebase, repository history, current default branch, branches, pull requests, plans, tests, CI, schemas, runtime paths, tools, prompts, memory systems, model backends, governance rules, and actual execution state of:

`kb4beast/hive-mind-os`

Treat the repository as one unified technical system and decision problem.

The intended product is a **self-operating AI operating system** that can:

- receive an objective;
- understand and decompose it;
- investigate the repository and relevant external evidence;
- design a solution;
- implement it in an isolated candidate;
- test and challenge it;
- integrate compatible work;
- recover from ordinary failure;
- learn from mistakes and outcomes;
- improve its prompts, curricula, retrieval, tools, plans, and non-authoritative code through governed challengers;
- and complete eligible missions end to end with no human interaction required.

The current concern is that the named roles may exist in documentation, enums, registrations, fixtures, or placeholder handlers without being genuinely connected to a shared runtime or capable of meaningful cognition, coordination, effects, learning, verification, and recovery.

The roles are:

1. Orchestrator
2. Explorer
3. Architect
4. Builder
5. Curator
6. Integrator
7. Steward
8. Optimizer

A major required behavior is **role-first resolution before human escalation**:

When any role believes that:

- it needs a human to answer a question;
- proceeding might be “cheating”;
- evidence may be contaminated;
- acceptance criteria may be unclear;
- authority may be insufficient;
- the current plan may be wrong;
- verification may not be independent;
- a tool action may be unsafe;
- or it cannot determine the next legitimate step;

it must not immediately stop and ask a human.

It must first invoke a typed, evidence-bound inter-role consultation process using the relevant roles. Other roles must be able to challenge the concern, provide missing context, reconstruct acceptance criteria, identify an authorized path, remand the work, or prove that true human authority is required.

However, role consultation must never be used to fabricate or substitute for:

- credentials or secrets;
- legal consent;
- business-owner value judgments;
- protected-branch authorization;
- independent human approval where policy genuinely requires a human;
- irreversible destructive authority;
- external facts that are unavailable;
- acceptance evidence;
- or any other authority the roles do not possess.

The target is **humanless-by-default operation inside sealed, pre-authorized boundaries**, not false claims that software can invent external authority.

# REQUIRED TRUTH STANDARD

## Repository reality outranks narrative

Inspect the current repository and GitHub state. Verify the exact current target branch and SHA before relying on any historical claim.

Do not blindly trust:

- plans;
- handoffs;
- PR descriptions;
- comments;
- status reports;
- architecture diagrams;
- generated receipts;
- role names;
- tests that do not exercise the runtime path;
- documentation that claims a feature is complete;
- branch names;
- PR titles;
- or stale execution-state files.

A capability exists only when the intended runtime can reach it and receipts prove it.

Distinguish:

- VERIFIED FACT;
- SOURCE-DERIVED CLAIM;
- INFERENCE;
- DESIGN RECOMMENDATION;
- PROPOSED IMPLEMENTATION;
- NOT RUN;
- BLOCKED;
- UNKNOWN.

Never claim that commands, tests, branches, commits, PRs, deployments, model calls, role executions, or side effects occurred unless an enabled tool produced verifiable receipts.

Missing evidence is a blocker, not permission to guess.

## Honest role identity

A single model performing multiple role passes is not eight independent humans or eight independently authenticated processes.

Do not present same-model labels as independent human review.

Procedural separation may still be useful, but independence claims must describe the actual isolation, context separation, candidate sealing, identity, and evidence boundaries.

## Honest autonomy

Do not use “fully autonomous” as one unqualified Boolean.

Measure and promote autonomy separately for at least:

- observation;
- analysis;
- planning;
- sandbox execution;
- repository changes;
- testing;
- delivery;
- recovery;
- learning;
- promotion;
- production effects.

Do not claim end-to-end autonomous operation until required mission suites, failure tests, authority checks, and recovery gates are proven.

- PARTIAL;

- FIXTURE_ONLY;

- DUPLICATED;

- ABSENT.

A role is not “wired” merely because:

- its name exists in an enum;
- it has a class;
- it returns JSON;
- it is present in a prompt;
- a fixture invokes it;
- or documentation describes it.

## 4. Required role behavior

The tournament and implementation must determine the strongest exact contracts, but the final system must provide meaningful capabilities equivalent to the following.

### Orchestrator

- translate objectives into typed missions;
- reconstruct acceptance criteria;
- propose a dynamic work graph;
- identify dependencies, budgets, risks, critical path, conflict surfaces, and stop conditions;
- request role consultations;
- replan when evidence changes;
- preserve customer-value traceability;
- never remove required verification or broaden authority.

### Explorer

- inspect the repository and authorized external sources;
- maintain source identity and provenance;
- compare approaches;
- identify uncertainty and evidence gaps;
- extract atomic claims;
- produce evidence and opportunity maps;
- distinguish source evidence from recommendations.

### Architect

- define interfaces, invariants, state ownership, data flow, failure modes, threat model, migration, compatibility, rollback, and acceptance mapping;
- challenge duplicate architecture;
- provide implementable decisions rather than vague design language.

### Builder

- work only in an isolated exact-candidate workspace;
- implement bounded changes;
- honor path ownership;
- create tests;
- emit patch, commit, tree, command, and evidence receipts;
- never modify the caller’s live worktree or protected branch directly;
- request explicit effects rather than silently performing unauthorized effects.

### Curator

- reconstruct acceptance independently in a fresh context;
- seal acceptance before candidate access when independence matters;
- design adversarial verification;
- map every material claim to evidence;
- detect leakage, self-grading, future knowledge, and metric gaming;
- remand, reject, quarantine, or recommend acceptance;
- never present Builder self-assessment as independent proof.

### Integrator

- verify interface, schema, version, migration, lineage, and runtime compatibility;
- detect duplicate canonical runtimes;
- reconcile parallel work;
- prove no intended change was lost;
- validate branch convergence and integration receipts;
- remand implementation defects instead of silently changing acceptance.

### Steward

- define and inspect health, SLOs, observability, operational risk, evidence retention, capacity, quotas, incidents, runbooks, and recovery behavior;
- analyze failures;
- propose bounded repairs;
- never bypass deterministic recovery policy.

### Optimizer

- measure outcomes and human-correction burden;
- cluster errors;
- propose prompt, curriculum, retrieval, tool, planner, skill, and code challengers;
- maintain diverse candidate archives;
- define holdouts and experiment budgets;
- request promotion;
- stop at diminishing returns;
- never evaluate or promote its own challenger.

Roles should be invoked dynamically based on mission need. Do not force every mission through a ceremonial fixed sequence when some passes add no value.

# PART III — ROLE-FIRST RESOLUTION BEFORE HUMAN ESCALATION

## 5. Inventory every human dependency

Search code, docs, tests, workflows, issues, and runtime behavior for:

- interactive prompts;
- `input()` or equivalent;
- manual approval comments;
- TODOs requiring a person;
- placeholder exceptions;
- `NotImplemented`;
- “ask user” branches;
- ambiguous error states;
- manual recovery steps;
- manual branch or PR tracking;
- manual model selection;
- manual conflict resolution;
- manual verification;
- manual promotion;
- missing secrets;
- missing tools;
- unhandled authority questions.

Classify each dependency as exactly one of:

1. `ROLE_RESOLVABLE` — another role can answer from available evidence.
2. `DETERMINISTICALLY_RESOLVABLE` — code or policy can resolve it without an LLM.
3. `TOOL_RESOLVABLE` — an already-authorized tool can fetch or perform what is needed.
4. `EVIDENCE_BLOCKED` — evidence is unavailable and cannot be inferred.
5. `ENVIRONMENT_BLOCKED` — required runtime, service, or dependency is unavailable.
6. `CREDENTIAL_BLOCKED` — an external credential is required.
7. `AUTHORITY_REQUIRED` — a genuine owner or external authority decision is required.
8. `POLICY_PROHIBITED` — the requested action is not eligible.
9. `BUG` — human involvement exists only because of a defect.
10. `UNCLASSIFIED` — insufficient evidence; must be investigated.

The implementation goal is to eliminate every avoidable human dependency.

## 6. Implement a typed consultation protocol

Before a role emits `HUMAN_REQUIRED`, it must create a `RoleConsultationRequest`.

At minimum, the request must include:

- consultation ID;
- mission ID;
- work-item ID;
- originating role and version;
- exact question;
- exact reason human help appears necessary;
- whether a “cheating” concern exists;
- concern category;
- evidence references;
- known facts;
- disputed claims;
- missing information;
- current authority envelope;
- actions already attempted;
- candidate resolutions;
- requested responder roles;
- maximum consultation rounds;
- timeout/budget;
- stopping condition.

Each responder emits a `RoleConsultationDecision` containing:

- responder role and version;
- answer or challenge;
- atomic claims;
- evidence references;
- confidence;
- applicable policy or precedent;
- whether the concern is valid;
- whether a safe authorized path exists;
- required remand or graph patch;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- recommended disposition.

Allowed dispositions:

- `PROCEED`;
- `PROCEED_WITH_CONSTRAINTS`;
- `REMAND`;
- `REPLAN`;
- `REQUEST_MORE_EVIDENCE`;
- `QUARANTINE`;
- `HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`;
- `POLICY_PROHIBITED`.

## 7. Route consultations intelligently

At minimum, use relevant combinations such as:

- requirements ambiguity:
  - Orchestrator;
  - Architect;
  - Curator.

- missing research or repository facts:
  - Explorer;
  - Curator;
  - relevant domain role.

- implementation uncertainty:
  - Architect;
  - Builder;
  - Integrator.

- verification or “cheating” concern:
  - Curator;
  - Steward;
  - Orchestrator;
  - relevant producing role excluded from sole judgment.

- side-effect or authority concern:
  - Steward;
  - Curator;
  - Orchestrator.

- integration conflict:
  - Integrator;
  - Architect;
  - affected Builders.

- self-improvement or promotion:
  - Optimizer;
  - Curator;
  - Steward;
  - an evaluator that did not produce the challenger.

The controller should select responders dynamically rather than hard-code one universal council.

## 8. Define “cheating” precisely

Do not let “cheating” remain a vague feeling.

Detect at least:

- acceptance criteria changed after candidate access;
- protected holdout leakage;
- future-commit or future-outcome leakage;
- evaluator saw Builder scratchpad;
- candidate authored its own supposedly independent acceptance test;
- same actor is presented as independent reviewer;
- self-assessment is used as sole proof;
- metric gaming;
- fabricated, stale, or mismatched receipts;
- evidence copied from a different commit/tree;
- use of the live worktree when isolation was required;
- unauthorized protected-branch mutation;
- hidden API billing fallback;
- authority encoded only in prompt text;
- model output directly mutating authoritative state;
- suppressed dissent or failed tests;
- unlicensed copying;
- retrying until a random favorable answer appears;
- unbounded search or self-replication;
- redefining the mission to make success easier.

The consultation process may determine that an action is legitimate, but it cannot alter sealed acceptance or grant missing authority.

## 9. Prevent consultation theater

The system must prevent:

- infinite role-to-role loops;
- consensus treated as proof;
- all roles sharing the same contaminated context;
- the originating role selecting only friendly reviewers;
- fake independence;
- duplicated questions;
- stale consultations;
- role collusion to weaken acceptance;
- escalation avoidance when genuine human authority is required.

Use:

- bounded rounds;
- deterministic routing rules;
- conflict-of-interest metadata;
- fresh-context review where necessary;
- evidence requirements;
- dissent retention;
- timeout and circuit breakers;
- explicit unresolved state;
- deterministic policy gates after consultation.

A council decision is advisory unless the deterministic controller has authority to enact it.

## 10. Human escalation packet

Only after consultation and deterministic checks fail may the system emit a human escalation.

The escalation must be a complete packet containing:

- exact blocked objective;
- current candidate and state;
- why roles and tools could not resolve it;
- consultation transcript references;
- evidence;
- options;
- consequences;
- recommended narrow decision;
- reversibility;
- default safe action;
- what work can continue without the answer;
- exact authority needed.

Do not ask a human broad or avoidable questions.

# PART IV — AUTONOMY, LEARNING, MEMORY, AND SELF-HEALING

## 11. Define the autonomous mission loop

The target runtime must support a governed loop equivalent to:

`objective -> charter -> evidence -> dynamic work graph -> role work -> consultation -> effects -> receipts -> verification -> integration -> outcome -> learning -> recovery/replan -> completion`

Every transition must have:

- typed input;
- explicit authority;
- deterministic validation where possible;
- version binding;
- evidence;
- retry policy;
- timeout;
- stopping condition;
- rollback or quarantine path.

## 12. Separate deterministic control from probabilistic cognition

Do not predeclare a winner, but aggressively compare architectures that separate:

### Deterministic responsibilities

- mission and acceptance state;
- event append;
- projection and replay;
- graph validity;
- leases;
- permissions and capability tokens;
- budgets and quotas;
- effect outbox;
- idempotency;
- evidence binding;
- candidate identity;
- verification gates;
- promotion;
- rollback;
- incidents;
- quarantine;
- recovery policy.

### Probabilistic responsibilities

- analysis;
- research synthesis;
- architecture proposals;
- work decomposition proposals;
- code generation;
- graph-patch proposals;
- tool/effect proposals;
- diagnosis;
- lessons;
- curricula;
- prompt candidates;
- optimization ideas;
- recovery recommendations.

Test this against serious alternatives. Preserve the strongest design, not a favored label.

Regardless of architecture, model output must not silently become authority.

## 13. Learning loop

Every material success, failure, correction, recovery, and mission outcome should be eligible to create an immutable learning episode.

The system must distinguish causes such as:

- reasoning failure;
- missing context;
- bad retrieval;
- stale memory;
- tool failure;
- authority denial;
- effect uncertainty;
- acceptance ambiguity;
- environment incompatibility;
- quota exhaustion;
- code defect;
- evaluator defect;
- prompt injection;
- memory poisoning;
- metric gaming;
- role-handoff loss;
- planner defect;
- human preference.

A robust improvement flow should include:

1. failure or outcome observation;
2. root-cause hypotheses;
3. evidence collection;
4. deterministic reproduction where possible;
5. lesson candidate;
6. regression or evaluation case;
7. challenger creation;
8. fresh-context evaluation;
9. `KEEP`, `RETEST`, `DISCARD`, `QUARANTINE`, or `STOP`;
10. monitored promotion;
11. rollback on regression.

Never mutate the active champion in place.

## 14. Role learning cells

Investigate and tournament whether each role should contain separable functions equivalent to:

- Student/operator;
- Teacher/researcher;
- Critic/evaluator;
- prompt genome;
- curriculum;
- skill library;
- competency graph;
- memory policy;
- health state.

Do not let Teacher promote its own curriculum or Optimizer grade its own challenger.

Prompt learning may change strategy, retrieval, examples, tools, planner heuristics, and skills, but must not mutate constitution, authority, sealed acceptance, evidence boundaries, or promotion burden.

## 15. Memory

Design memory that is:

- logically durable;
- append-only for evidence and decisions;
- content-addressed where useful;
- provenance-preserving;
- temporally valid;
- contradiction-aware;
- supersession-aware;
- access-controlled;
- replayable;
- partitioned from protected holdouts;
- bounded in active context;
- searchable through lexical, semantic, graph, temporal, and provenance indexes;
- capable of retaining raw evidence without treating every raw transcript as truth.

At minimum distinguish:

- EVIDENCE;
- FACT;
- EPISODE;
- OPINION;
- LESSON;
- WORKING;
- SCRATCHPAD;
- SELF_ASSESSMENT;
- PRECEDENT;
- CURRICULUM;
- SKILL;
- COMPETENCY;
- INCIDENT.

The runtime, not an unconstrained role prompt, should build the retrieval manifest.

## 16. Self-healing

Compare and implement the strongest bounded recovery design.

Known failure classes should be handled deterministically when possible.

A recovery ladder should consider:

1. bounded transient retry;
2. fresh process or model session;
3. rematerialization from immutable inputs;
4. projection rebuild from events;
5. effect reconciliation through idempotency and external observation;
6. already-authorized equivalent adapter;
7. rollback to previous candidate, prompt, graph, or component;
8. quarantine;
9. Steward diagnosis;
10. narrow human escalation only when genuinely required.

Add circuit breakers for:

- repeated malformed output;
- repeated provider failure;
- repeated effect uncertainty;
- candidate identity drift;
- receipt mismatch;
- memory poisoning;
- nondeterministic verification;
- budget exhaustion;
- no-progress recovery loops;
- consultation loops.

An LLM may diagnose an unknown failure, but must not close a circuit without a deterministic probe or required evidence.

## 17. Durable effects

No role should directly perform an untracked external side effect.

Investigate and implement an effect lifecycle such as:

`intent -> policy check -> authorized -> executing -> observed -> receipted -> reconciled`

Each effect should bind:

- exact inputs;
- actor;
- authority;
- adapter and version;
- candidate/work item;
- idempotency key;
- timeout;
- retry class;
- external observation;
- partial-failure state;
- receipt;
- compensation or rollback when possible.

# PART VI — TURN THE WINNER INTO AN EXECUTABLE PROGRAM

## 25. Do not stop at a document

Reverse-engineer the winner into a repository-resident implementation control plane.

This implementation control plane coordinates the work required to build Hive Mind OS. It is not automatically the product’s canonical runtime. Keep those concerns separate and prevent accidental duplicate architecture.

The human operator should not have to remember:

- which node ran;
- which branch merged;
- which PR is open;
- what changed outside the plan;
- which dependencies completed;
- which work can run in parallel;
- which model is sufficient;
- whether repair or reconciliation is needed;
- which integration wave is next;
- when the plan must change.

## 26. Use a dynamic dependency graph

Use the execution structure that best fits the implementation. For complex repository work, strongly prefer a dynamic dependency DAG unless evidence supports another structure.

Do not force artificial breadth-first waves.

A node becomes executable immediately when its true dependencies and safety conditions are satisfied.

Execute the maximum safe dependency-ready set in parallel.

The graph may be changed when evidence reveals:

- missing dependencies;
- wrong assumptions;
- duplicate work;
- hidden blockers;
- newly available parallelism;
- obsolete nodes;
- architecture conflicts;
- safer decomposition;
- better implementation paths.

Maintain an append-only reason for every graph change.

## 27. Machine-readable node contract

Each node must define, where relevant:

- immutable node ID;
- objective;
- rationale;
- dependencies;

- unresolved loops;

- defect escape;

- recovery;

## 44. No-cheating acceptance

- a challenger is immutable and derived from a champion;

- holdout and regression suites exist;
- improvement exceeds noise or is marked `RETEST`;
- guardrails pass;
- promotion and rollback receipts exist;
- overfitting and regression are detected;

- stale lease;

- provider unavailable;
- authentication expiry;

- partial Git write;

- candidate mutation;
- poisoned memory;
- evaluator leakage;
- repeated no-progress recovery.

The system must recover, remand, roll back, or quarantine predictably. It must not retry forever.

## 47. Integration and repository safety

Free-think.

Do not anchor on current architecture.

Do not confuse registration with wiring.

Do not confuse JSON output with intelligence.

Do not confuse consensus with proof.

Do not confuse local tests with release readiness.

Do not confuse historical evidence with evidence for the current tree.

Do not hide weaknesses.

Do not average away catastrophic failures.

Do not manufacture consensus.

Do not force breadth-first execution.

Do not force a predetermined architecture.

Do not force irrelevant technologies.

Do not use LLMs for deterministic bookkeeping.

Do not let LLM output directly grant authority.

Do not let roles avoid a solvable problem by asking a human.

Do not let roles fabricate a human answer when external authority is genuinely required.

Do not let an Optimizer promote its own challenger.

Do not allow hidden self-modification.

Do not allow unbounded retry or self-replication.

Do not allow concealed activity.

Do not expose credentials.

Do not auto-merge.

Preserve dissent, rejected alternatives, failed attempts, unresolved evidence, and rollback paths.

Optimize for verified end-to-end value, truthful autonomy, reversibility, evidence completeness, low avoidable human burden, and a system that improves without weakening its own contract.

The objective is not to make Hive Mind OS appear autonomous.

The objective is to make the strongest reasonably discoverable form of Hive Mind OS measurably, verifiably, and operationally autonomous within its legitimate authority.
