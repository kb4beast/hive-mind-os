# EXF-050 — Round two: adversarial battles

**Node:** `EXF-050` · **Round:** R5 · **Roles:** Architect, Curator
**Stopping condition:** every leading entrant has faced a scenario built to defeat it and the outcome is recorded with a fundamental-or-fixable verdict.

Each surviving entrant is attacked with the scenario most likely to kill it. Triple
elimination is the point: an entrant is not removed for one narrow weakness, only for a
weakness that is **fundamental** — one that cannot be engineered around within the
programme's reach.

---

## T1 · Corrosion-evidence coating

**Attack A — "The patent is not yours to have."**
NASA's KSC corrosion coating may be exclusively licensed, dormant, or unavailable in
2026. Without the license the entrant collapses to a software wrapper.
→ **Fundamental if true, but it is a fact, not a flaw.** It is checkable in one enquiry
through the NASA T2 portal and, more directly, through ExF's own Space Act Agreement —
which is precisely the service ExF exists to provide. Carried as blocking confirmation
`C-1`. If it fails, an equivalent NASA corrosion-sensing or coatings technology
substitutes; if none exists, T1 degrades to T3.

**Attack B — "Industrial coatings is a commodity market owned by giants."**
PPG, AkzoNobel, Sherwin-Williams and Hempel own coastal and marine protective coatings.
A South Texas startup does not win on coating chemistry.
→ **Fixable, and the attack actually sharpens the entry.** The entrant should not sell
coating chemistry. The coating is the *sensor*; the product is the **inspection record**:
a defensible, tamper-evident corrosion history per asset, per inspection, per date. The
incumbents sell a can. This sells an auditable answer to "prove the asset was sound on
this date" — which is what insurers, regulators and asset buyers actually pay for.
Repositioned, the giants become channel partners rather than competitors.

**Attack C — "Coating qualification takes years."**
Getting into an owner's approved-products specification is a multi-year process.
→ **Real wound, partially fixable.** Mitigation: enter through inspection pilots on
secondary structures and existing coated assets, where the indicator is applied as a
monitoring layer rather than replacing the protective system. Revenue starts as
inspection services, not as coating sales. This is the entrant's largest genuine risk
and it is recorded as such rather than argued away.

**Verdict: advances, wounded on Attack C, blocked on confirmation C-1.**

## T3 · Compliance evidence platform

**Attack A — "You are selling surveillance to the surveilled."**
The obvious buyer is the operator whose emissions and noise you are measuring. Operators
do not buy independent, tamper-evident evidence of their own performance. Regulators
would buy it but procure slowly and rarely from startups.
→ **Fundamental in the regulator framing; fixable in the operator framing, but not
comfortably.** Operators do want cheap, defensible, audit-ready record-keeping — the
alternative is hand-assembled data packages and enforcement exposure. But the pitch has
an inherent awkwardness that T1 does not: T1 sells an asset owner *protection*, T3 sells
them *proof that can be used against them*.

**Attack B — "Political risk for the sponsor."**
Launch-site environmental monitoring is contested locally. A non-profit whose ecosystem
depends on industry goodwill may not want to champion it.
→ **Fixable by framing** — position it as compliance cost reduction, not as watchdog
technology — **but the framing risk is permanent.**

**Verdict: advances as the runner-up profile. Strong mechanism, uncomfortable buyer.**

## T2 · Cryogenic boil-off recovery

**Attack — "SpaceX will build it themselves."**
The anchor customer is the most aggressive vertical integrator in the industry, and is
already building its own liquefaction plant at the site. Selling cryogenic efficiency to
SpaceX is selling into their core competence.
→ **Fundamental.** The escape is to target LNG export terminals and industrial gas
instead — but then the space leg weakens, and the entrant is a capital-equipment startup
competing with established EPC contractors on their home ground, with a 2/10 on capital
efficiency and no ability to demonstrate anything at a pitch.

**Verdict: advances on domain fit alone. Loss is fundamental, not fixable at this scale.**

## T10 · Space-weather grid alerting

**Attack — "NOAA gives it away and the incumbents are entrenched."**
NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center publishes free products; commercial providers
already serve utilities; ERCOT procurement is slow and conservative.
→ **Fundamental.** Free baseline plus entrenched vendors plus slow procurement is three
walls, and the entrant has no regional anchor to compensate (regional impact 4/10).

**Verdict: eliminated.**

---

## T5 · Hydrogen-sensing tape

**Attack — "A component is not a company."**
The technology has been licensed before (to UCF), regional hydrogen demand is
speculative rather than present, and sensing tape is a part someone else designs into a
system.
→ **Fixable in principle, thin in practice.** No regional pull, and the entrant would be
a supplier to markets that do not yet exist in the Rio Grande Valley.

**Verdict: eliminated.**

---

## T7 · Composite cryogenic valve

**Attack — "Two customers, both of whom build in-house, and a multi-year qualification."**
Flight and energy service qualification is long and capital-heavy; the precedent of the
NASA valve reaching a methane coal-gas field is encouraging but took institutional
backing the entrant does not have.
→ **Fundamental at this funding scale**, despite an excellent 9/10 NASA fit and 10/10
domain fit. A prize cannot fund a valve qualification programme.

**Verdict: eliminated.**

## Attacking the leader on purpose

The tournament's own conclusion was attacked before being accepted:

> *"T1 wins only because the rubric was written to reward NASA licensing and regional
> impact, and those weights were chosen by the same process that picked T1."*

This is the strongest objection to the whole exercise and it deserves a direct answer.
The weights were fixed in `03_EVALUATION_SYSTEM.md` **before** any candidate was scored,
and each one is traced to a specific observed funding event — DOE's prize naming Rockets
& Rigs, SBA's Capital Formation category, the Space Act Agreement, the pitch-competition
format. The rubric is also demonstrably not tuned to flatter the applicant: it **vetoed
the applicant's own existing product (T8)** in round one. A rubric built to justify a
predetermined answer would not have eliminated the one entrant the applicant already
owns.

**Three survivors advance: T1, T3, T2.**
