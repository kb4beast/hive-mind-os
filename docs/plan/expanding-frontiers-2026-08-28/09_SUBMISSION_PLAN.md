# EXF-080 — Submission plan: turning the winner into an actual entry

**Node:** `EXF-080` · **Round:** R8 · **Role:** Orchestrator
**Stopping condition:** the winner has a named target program, a first action, and an explicit list of operator confirmations required before submitting.

## Target program

**Primary route — NASA Tech Trek → ExF Space Tech Pitch Competition.**

This is the correct door, not merely an available one. Tech Trek is the six-month program
run under ExF's Space Act Agreement with NASA Johnson Space Center, in which ExF acts as
the technology-transfer concierge; the pitch competition is historically won by its
graduates commercialising licensed NASA technologies. The winning entry is a licensed
NASA technology commercialised in Brownsville. It belongs in that cohort.

**Secondary route — Rockets & Rigs.** Present the same entry as a cross-industry
corrosion problem shared by launch infrastructure and oil & gas. This is where ExF's DOE
money came from, and it is the framing most likely to attract an energy-side partner.

**Do not lead with the ExF Accelerator.** It is a generalist on-ramp; the two routes above
are specific to what this entry is.

## First action

Contact Expanding Frontiers and ask one question before anything else:

> *"Under your NASA JSC Space Act Agreement, can you check availability of the Kennedy
> Space Center smart corrosion-detection and inhibition coating — 'Coatings and Methods
> for Corrosion Detection and/or Reduction', KSC-TOPS-1 — for license, and tell me which
> current program is the right entry point for commercialising it in Brownsville?"*

That single question does three things at once: it confirms the fact the entire verdict
depends on (`R-1`), it uses ExF's own service in the way ExF wants it used, and it opens
the conversation as a specific technology-transfer request rather than a generic
"how do I apply".

## What to build before the pitch

Sequenced so that the cheapest thing that can kill the entry is tested first.

1. **Confirm `C-1`.** Nothing else is worth doing until the license question is answered.
2. **Salt-fog coupons.** Coated and uncoated samples exposed in a marine-equivalent
   cabinet, showing visible early corrosion indication. A university lab can run this.
3. **The inspection receipt.** Extend the existing evidence engine so an inspection —
   asset ID, date, photograph, sealed check, independent re-execution — produces the same
   tamper-evident bundle the repository already generates for code. This is an adaptation
   of working software, not new invention.
4. **One named local asset.** A specific pipeline segment, port structure, or pad
   component, with a named owner willing to host a pilot. One real asset beats a market
   size slide.
5. **The follow-on slide.** NASA SBIR as technology infusion on a licensed NASA
   technology; DoD Corrosion Prevention and Control; DOE SBIR on pipeline and storage
   integrity. Judges from a non-profit accelerator want to know where the company goes
   after their prize.

## How to open the pitch

Put the two coupons on the table before speaking. Say what the panel is looking at, then
show the receipt that binds it. Then, and only then, explain that the coating is NASA's,
the corrosion is Brownsville's, and the record is the business.

Say the qualification-timeline weakness out loud, in your own words, before a judge finds
it. A panel of industry operators will already know that specification adoption takes
years; naming it and explaining the inspection-services entry path converts the single
biggest objection into evidence that you understand the industry.
