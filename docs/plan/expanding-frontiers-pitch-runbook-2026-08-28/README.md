## The four decks

| Deck | Idea | Elevator pitch | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Boom Baseline** | Every major airport continuously measures its own noise. Not one spaceport does. | $750K pre-seed, or a $250K design-partner contract |
| 2 | **Peninsula** | A $12B LNG export terminal and a methane-fuelled orbital launch site now share one peninsula, and no model exists for what one does to the other. | $500K, or two paid pilot studies |
| 3 | **SARGO** | The Gulf took 5 million metric tons of sargassum in a single month, Texas pays to shovel it off the beach, and 30 km away is a launch site that runs on methane. | $1.5M seed, or $400K non-dilutive to kill the arsenic question |
| 4 | **The Boom Map** | Every airport measures its noise. No spaceport does. I built the first one, on my street. | Mentorship, an internship, and $2K of sensors |

## Two rules the whole runbook is built on

**Say the weak thing first.** Each idea has one genuine hole and each deck names it on
its own slide: Boom Baseline has no confirmed payer, Peninsula looks like consulting,
SARGO has arsenic, The Boom Map is a high school project. An investor who finds an
undisclosed hole stops trusting the true parts of the deck too.

**Grade every number, and cut every C.** A, B or DERIVED goes on a slide. C does not.
The market sizing is deliberately a ladder of graded figures rather than a single TAM,
because a fabricated TAM is the fastest way to lose a technical investor, and
"the analogous market is $343M a year and spaceport spend is zero" is a stronger
sentence than an invented multiplication.
